  • Build a large, realistic training set
  • Design an architecture suited to your data
  • Handle data preprocessing/normalization
  • Train the network on a few GPUs (about a day)
  • Apply the network to your problem

“Any-to-any” modeling with generative masking

  • Training pairs observations of the same objects from different instruments.
  • The model is trained with cross-modal generative masking (Mizrahi et al. 2023)
    => It learns the joint and all conditional distributions of the modalities:  
\forall m,n \quad p(x_m | x_n)

The AION-1 model family

  • Models trained as part of the GENCI Jean Zay Grand Challenges 4 (2024), following the extension of Jean Zay with a new partition of 1,400 H100 GPUs
  • AION-1 Base: 300M parameters

  • AION-1 XLarge: 3B parameters

Foundation models as research infrastructure

  • Foundation models as shared research infrastructure, like our software infrastructure
    • Like Astropy or NumPy: built and maintained by a community, used by everyone
    • Reusable tools: trained once on a supercomputer, adapted by many in minutes on a single GPU
    • Changes the dynamics of deep learning in science: effort shifts to asking science questions
  • Requires data infrastructure to support them
    • Standardized access to data, not just catalogs, across surveys
    • Curated, open multimodal datasets (e.g. Multimodal Universe); embeddings shipped with data releases
  • Requires coordinated compute and model building
    • Training needs national-scale HPC (e.g. Jean Zay: 1,400 H100 GPUs), pooled across the community
    • A few well-maintained open models with standard APIs, versioning and long-term support
    • Community benchmarks to track progress across science cases
